Assesing Online C++ Sources

What makes a good online source:
- Follow this rule of thumb:
 -- Do you feel more capable after reading it?

 -Other Indicators:
 -- Accurate
 -- Clearly explains the context
 -- Gives Depth with the explanation
 -- Gives practical examples
 -- Provides limitations with examples
 -- Up to date and maintained regularly
 -- No Hidden Agendas, such as trying to sell you an online course
 -- Accessible

What makes bad online sources:

-Indicators
 -- Clickbait titles
 -- Doesn't provide good examples
 -- Writes code with absolutes (Never use this technique)
 -- Old information
 -- Tries to sell you something
 -- Huge/Long Intros

List of good resources:
-W3Schools.com
 -- https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/cpp/c-plus-plus/
 -- Great at giving the basic documentation on how to use things like iterators

-GeeksfoGeeks.com
 -- https://www.w3schools.com/CPP/
 -- Great at giving the basic documentation, just another resource like W3Schools.com

-Chrno's C++ youtube series
 -- https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLlrATfBNZ98dudnM48yfGUldqGD0S4FFb
 -- Great for showing you how to understanding the basics, from setting it up to more complex techniques.

-cpprefrence
 -- https://en.cppreference.com/
 -- All the refrences are easily visible, and although the page looks like it was designed 15 years ago,
    I love the interface and ease of use. Highly recommend just have this page up while starting to search,
    it had literally everything I needed for all assignments in the CSE220C (Intro to C++) class.

-Claude AI 
 -- Claude.ai
 -- This is my favorite because Claude will go through most of the documentation for you, as well as going through
    all of StackOverflow, Reddit and other forum-esque sites so you don't have to.
    You can also ask it very specific questions, such as iterator implemntation and different syntax to help.
    Be careful not to rely on it too much though, as it can easily solve most programming problems/assignemnts 
    with ease. Make sure to use it as a learning tool, rather than using it to complete assignments for you

List of okay resources:
-Stack Overflow
 -- This used to be considered a great resource, but with the advent of AI programs, it has become more 
    obselete simply because any AI can pour through thousands of pages in a few seconds and give you the 
    best result.
    Only "Okay" because often the solution is from 10+ years ago and is most likely only slightly related, 
    using programming techniques considered archaic and/or bad design. You might get lucky and have someone
    asking about the exact thing you are working on. 

-Reddit
 -- Less reliable than Stack, and often most people on reddit will not be able to answer the question properly.
    Much like Stack, the questions were often asked 5+ years ago and the answer may not be helpful or apply to
    what you are working on. I only use reddit if I have exhausted pretty much all other avenues.
